# Staging feedback plan

## Purpose

Use a staging release to find confusing questions, weak matches, missing comparison details, and data errors. Scheduled user interviews are not a prerequisite. Feedback can improve usability and recommendations, but it does not by itself prove commercial demand.

## Lightweight approach

- Invite a small, voluntary group through available project or community channels; aim for people who may be considering an EV, without treating the group as representative.
- Let visitors try the journey on their own time. Offer the form at the results screen and through a persistent feedback link.
- Review responses and observed issues regularly. Record the issue, evidence, severity, and whether it was fixed; avoid treating one opinion as a universal preference.
- Do not ask for a name, email, exact address, or precise travel history by default. Make any contact field optional and separate from feedback.

## Short feedback form

1. **Clarity:** Were the questions and results easy to understand? What was unclear?
2. **Usefulness:** Did the options help you compare cars for your needs? Why or why not?
3. **Missing information:** What information would you need before taking a next step?
4. **Trust:** How much did you trust the information and its sources? What affected your trust?
5. **Incorrect data:** Did you spot a fact that looks incorrect or out of date? Share the model, field, and a source or note if available.

An optional final prompt may ask what the visitor would change. Keep free-text answers voluntary.

## Observable signals

Use aggregate counts where possible, with a short retention period and no unnecessary identifiers:

- Questionnaire starts and completions, including where visitors leave.
- Results displayed, no-match outcomes, and whether visitors open source links.
- Feedback form submissions and issue categories, including incorrect-data reports.
- Basic reliability signals such as errors preventing a completed journey.

Interpret these as clues about the staging experience, not as proof of market size, willingness to pay, or purchase intent. Avoid collecting precise location, detailed trip routes, or other sensitive data unless a specific need is established.

## Review and follow-up

Before staging, check that source links and last-checked dates are visible and that the feedback form works. During staging, review feedback and aggregate signals at a regular team cadence, verify reported facts against their sources, and prioritize fixes that affect trust or fit. After the feedback period, summarize what was observed, what changed, remaining uncertainty, and what should be tested next.
